## What Buurtzorg actually is

Jos de Blok, Gonnie Kronenberg, and Ard Leferink founded Buurtzorg in 2006 as neighborhood nursing in the Netherlands. The unit of the firm is not the nurse, not the region, and not a department. The unit is a self-managing team of at most twelve, serving a named neighborhood, owning intake, scheduling, hiring, quality, local budget, and the office itself. When the team stably exceeds twelve, it splits. When it falls too small, teams merge. There is no team boss.

The rest of the firm is deliberately thin. Typical published shape: two directors as the only managerial posts, on the order of twenty coaches, and a back office of about fifty for payroll, invoicing, contracts, and ICT. Nurses are ~99.5% of headcount. Overhead in the Dutch studies sits near 8% against an industry average near 25%. Teams must keep a productivity floor — commonly cited as ≥62% of hours on billable patient care — so the node stays solvent without a manager optimizing utilization.

Coaches advise. They do not command. A coach covers on the order of forty to fifty teams so no team can become a client of the coach. Advice can be refused. The IT system (BuurtzorgWeb plus the Omaha taxonomy) is the substitute for coordination layers that would otherwise become middle management.

De Blok’s slogan is humanity over bureaucracy. The care model is an onion: the person at the center, then informal network (family, neighbors), then the team, then formal specialists at the outer ring. Professional time is what remains after the inner rings can hold the work. That is not charity theater. It is a rule for what the team is allowed to take on.

Citizen Gardens copies the topology, not the nursing license. That sentence is already bound in Governing Principles §3.2.

## What a Sovereignty Node actually owns

A node is a complete operational unit. It is not a reporting line.

It owns:

- Intake of work inside its envelope (garden hours, SUG households, reentry sprint slots, playground experiments).
- Scheduling and coverage.
- Hiring of additional operators up to the cap of twelve, by a within-node hiring circle that tests for self-management fitness, not pedigree.
- Quality: the public 90-day metric card.
- Local spend inside the envelope. Equipment ≤ $2,500 with association title. Anything above dual-control $2,500 or the material-asset floor ($5,000 / vehicle / lease >12 months / land) leaves the node and goes to the association process.
- The decision to split when the cap is stably breached.

It does not own:

- Civic L0. Those nine invariants are association law, not local taste (L0-1 no member profit distribution; L0-4 no administrator binds beyond written authority; L0-8 credits do not overweight votes).
- The membership list (L0-3).
- The right to offer Phase Mirror as a managed service (L0-5). That is the operator’s licensed rail.
- Examiner authority. Equity and board seats at the operator cannot sit as Examiner.
- A fifth capital door. Node envelopes are filled through gift, sponsor-class membership, recoverable grant, or operator remittance. “We don’t want a big return” is not a membership investment.

Until first assembly, signers of the Principles are administrators. Their written authority is EIN, agent, register, counsel, and contracts under $2,500. That is already a Buurtzorg-shaped back office. It must not grow a regional manager.

## Onion model, civic version

Buurtzorg’s onion is care. Citizen Gardens’ onion is sovereignty of a neighborhood practice.

1. Person at the center. Consent withdrawable; withdrawal logged, not argued (L0-7). Youth needs confirmed guardian consent (L0-6).
2. Informal network. Household, block, garden plot, mutual aid. Reciprocity is counted as social energy. A mutual act doubles the interaction. Multiplicity of a member-state is \(M = 2R + 1\). Credits never buy votes.
3. Sovereignty Node ≤ 12. Professional time is what the inner rings cannot hold.
4. Association commons. Treasury, records, ICT, legal agent. Overhead ≤ 15%.
5. Outer specialists. Operator LLC for the hosted oracle. Counsel. Insurer. Partner QPU only when UAC leaves research. These do not sit inside the node.

Do not invert the onion. Do not staff the outer ring first and call it a department.

## Roles inside the twelve

Buurtzorg distributes seven rotating roles on top of the primary craft. Citizen Gardens can copy the pattern without copying the names as titles.

Primary role: the craft the node exists to do (garden work, SUG household support, 30–60 day reentry sprint, UCC completion-kernel delivery, playground experiment). Everyone holds this. A commons-only operator who never touches the craft is how overhead dies.

Distributed roles, rotating, no manager grade:

- Housekeeper — site, kit-of-parts, association title on tools.
- Planner — schedule and coverage.
- Reporter — 90-day metric card and dissonance register.
- Mentor — onboarding; never Examiner if they hold ≥10% of the operator or a board seat.
- Vitality — three-way test watch (dignity, outcome, solvency).
- Treasury liaison — spends only inside the envelope; dual control above $2,500 is an admin function, not a node throne.
- Network — informal-ring and neighboring-node contact.

Rotation twice a year is the Buurtzorg habit. Keep it. Permanence of a coordination role is how a manager grows without a title.

## Coaches are not a hidden hierarchy

A coach’s only job is to help a stuck node run the Phase Mirror loop: Extract, Map, Rank, Produce levers with owner / metric / horizon, one precision question if blocking.

A coach does not:

- Assign work.
- Hire or fire.
- Approve spend.
- Veto care-work or garden practice.
- Sit as Examiner while holding operator equity ≥10% or a board seat.

Coach load is a safety parameter. Buurtzorg keeps the ratio high (~40–50 teams per coach) so dependence cannot form. Year one, Citizen Gardens will have too few nodes for that ratio. The risk is the inverse: one coach and two nodes looks like a boss. Bind it in writing: coach huddle is biweekly and advisory only. Published advice. Refusable. Logged.

## Scaling law

Buurtzorg scaled from one team to on the order of a thousand teams by refusing to scale a pyramid. New teams form by proposal from practitioners who want a neighborhood, not by HQ planting a branch manager.

Citizen Gardens already encoded the same law:

- Role inside an existing node: node consent; envelope must cover 90 days.
- New place or new department: members under the Principles; named sponsor + 90-day metric; no hire before the envelope.
- Split when active operators stably exceed twelve.
- Pause after two failed quarters on the three-way test. Pause is a kill-switch, not a punishment.

SUG target is ≥ 300 households per mature node-year. That is the outer measure of a node that has earned the right to exist. It is not a reason to grow past twelve. Twelve operators serving three hundred households is the mature shape. Past twelve, split the neighborhood.

Do not create a “regional node.” That is a manager with a garden name.

## Overhead arithmetic

Buurtzorg’s 8% is an observed result of deleting coordination work, not an efficiency program layered on a hierarchy. Citizen Gardens binds ≤ 15%. Treat 15% as a ceiling, 8% as the reference.

Overhead is commons roles that do not touch node craft: records, treasury, ICT, legal agent, insurance administration, coach time.

Tests:

- If a proposed commons hire does not reduce node administrative load by more than its cost, it fails.
- If coach time exceeds published advice hours, it is becoming management. Cut it or split the coach role across practitioners.
- If the operator LLC’s staff is booked as UNA overhead, the two persons have collapsed. Remittance is a wire. Payroll of the shop is the shop’s.

Day-one dual control is $2,500. That is the first back-office control. It is not a reason to hire a controller.

## Named dissonances

These are permanent. Manage them. Do not declare them solved.

1. Shared craft vs mixed civic work. Buurtzorg teams share one licensed profession. A Citizen Gardens node may mix gardeners, reentry navigators, and UCC operators. Without a shared craft, self-management decays into vibes. Binding: each node names one primary craft on its 90-day card. Mixed work is a split condition, not a feature.
2. Insurance hours vs four doors. Buurtzorg solvency is billable care. Node solvency here is an envelope filled through one of four doors. A node with craft and no envelope is a volunteer club wearing a sovereignty name. Binding: no role before 90-day cover.
3. Coach scarcity vs coach capture. Too few nodes makes the coach a boss. Binding: advisory-only, published, refusable.
4. 15% cap vs 8% reference. Slack will be spent unless the commons publishes the ratio monthly.
5. Credentials vs no titles. PMCP attests practice. It does not create a node manager. Equity never mints a diploma. A certificate does not outrank consent inside the twelve.
6. Operator LLC vs node. Buurtzorg already split IT into a separate firm. Keep the wall. The shop remits. The garden does not employ the oracle as a department.
7. Informal-network onion vs privacy L0. Neighbors are the second ring. Cameras in a declared privacy zone are still forbidden (L0-2). Informal help is consented, not surveilled.
8. Split-as-success vs founder gravity. Founders will want one node that “holds the whole vision.” That is a department. Split by neighborhood or function when the cap is hit.
9. Computational contraction vs social topology. \(\|G\|_1 < 1.0\) and \(c < 1\) are not membership rules and not node standing orders. Do not print them on the 90-day card.

## What year one looks like if this infrastructure is real

Year-one product is UCC as a service — the completion kernel — not a UAC chemistry factory. The playground is Civic R&D as a live Sovereignty Node.

## How to read the set

Bushidō is duty inside the operator. Buurtzorg is the shape that makes that duty cheaper to keep.

- Gi and Jisei are why there is no manager. If the twelve cannot keep the few rules and govern themselves, the honest move is not a title. It is a pause or a split.
- Yū and Makoto are why Phase Mirror sits *inside* the node rather than above it. Name the contradiction. Put it on an artifact. Do not “align.”
- Jin and Rei are the onion. Care and personhood before professional capture. Civic L0-2, L0-6, L0-7 are the hard edge of Rei.
- Meiyo and Chūgi keep the two persons unmixed. Honor is inspectable work. Loyalty is to purpose. The operator LLC does not collect vassals. The UNA does not sell ranks.

Do not print these eight on a wall and call the node trained. A virtue without a binding artifact is costume. That is already the rule in §3.3.

## Named tension

Computational L0 — \(\|G\|_1 < 1.0\), \(c < 1\) — stays off this list. It is not a Bushidō virtue and not a Buurtzorg value.
